# Insights and Recommendations

## Purpose

This document translates the dashboard findings into business interpretation and practical HR recommendations.

The analysis is descriptive and diagnostic. It identifies workforce patterns and relationships associated with employee attrition, but it does not prove that any single factor caused employees to leave.

Recommendations should therefore be treated as areas for further investigation and structured intervention testing.

---

## 1. Overall Attrition

**Insight:**  
237 employees left from a workforce of 1,470, resulting in an attrition rate of 16.12%.

**Interpretation:**  
Attrition is significant enough to affect workforce stability, recruitment demand and knowledge retention.

**Recommendation:**  
Use the company attrition rate as a benchmark and track high-risk departments and roles separately.

---

## 2. Sales Has the Highest Departmental Attrition Rate

**Insight:**  
Sales recorded the highest department attrition rate at 20.63%.

**Interpretation:**  
Employees in Sales leave at a higher rate than employees in other departments.

**Recommendation:**  
Review workload, targets, incentives, manager support and career progression within Sales.

---

## 3. Research and Development Has the Highest Exit Volume

**Insight:**  
Research and Development contributed 133 exits, representing 56.12% of all employee exits.

**Interpretation:**  
Its attrition rate is lower than Sales, but its large workforce creates the highest number of exits.

**Recommendation:**  
Review high-volume roles within Research and Development, especially Laboratory Technicians.

---

## 4. Sales Representatives Are the Highest-Risk Job Role

**Insight:**  
Sales Representatives recorded the highest job-role attrition rate at approximately 39.76%.

**Interpretation:**  
This role shows a clear retention concern and requires deeper investigation.

**Recommendation:**  
Review compensation, targets, workload, travel, management support and career opportunities.

---

## 5. Laboratory Technicians Require Attention

**Insight:**  
Laboratory Technicians recorded high attrition and the highest number of exits among job roles.

**Interpretation:**  
The role combines elevated risk with a meaningful operational impact.

**Recommendation:**  
Review salary position, workload, training, progression and staffing conditions.

---

## 6. Overtime Is Strongly Associated With Attrition

**Insight:**  
Overtime employees recorded 30.5% attrition, compared with 10.4% for non-overtime employees.

**Interpretation:**  
The 20.1 percentage-point gap makes overtime one of the strongest observed attrition indicators.

**Recommendation:**  
Review repeated overtime, staffing capacity and workload in high-exposure roles.

---

## 7. Entry-Level Employees Have Higher Attrition

**Insight:**  
Entry-level employees recorded an attrition rate of approximately 26.3%.

**Interpretation:**  
Early career employees may face challenges related to onboarding, role clarity, support or progression.

**Recommendation:**  
Strengthen onboarding, mentoring, manager check-ins and career-path communication.

---

## 8. First-Year Attrition Is High

**Insight:**  
Employees with up to one year of tenure recorded attrition of approximately 34.9%.

**Interpretation:**  
Many exits occur early in the employee lifecycle.

**Recommendation:**  
Introduce retention check-ins at 30, 60, 90 days, six months and one year.

---

## 9. Low Job Satisfaction Is Linked With Higher Attrition

**Insight:**  
Employees with low job satisfaction recorded the highest attrition rate, while very high satisfaction recorded the lowest.

**Interpretation:**  
Low satisfaction may indicate unresolved workplace concerns.

**Recommendation:**  
Use targeted surveys and manager discussions to understand the main sources of dissatisfaction.

---

## 10. Poor Work-Life Balance Has the Highest Attrition

**Insight:**  
Employees reporting poor work-life balance recorded attrition of approximately 31.3%.

**Interpretation:**  
Poor work-life balance is strongly associated with employee exits, although the pattern is not perfectly linear across all groups.

**Recommendation:**  
Review workload, overtime, scheduling, travel and flexibility in affected teams.

---

## 11. Promotion Timing Shows a Non-Linear Pattern

**Insight:**  
Attrition was lowest among employees promoted three to five years ago and higher among recently promoted employees and those with promotion gaps of six to ten years.

**Interpretation:**  
Promotion alone does not guarantee retention, while long promotion gaps may reduce perceived career opportunity.

**Recommendation:**  
Provide post-promotion support and review career progression for employees with long promotion gaps.

---

## 12. Gender Is Not a Major Attrition Differentiator

**Insight:**  
Men contributed more exits, but male and female attrition rates were relatively similar.

**Interpretation:**  
The higher male exit count is mainly influenced by the larger male workforce.

**Recommendation:**  
Analyse gender together with role, department, income and overtime rather than treating it as a standalone risk factor.

---

## 13. Most Exits Occur in Lower Salary Bands

**Insight:**  
The majority of exits were recorded among employees earning up to 5K.

**Interpretation:**  
Lower salary groups contribute the largest exit volume, but this does not prove salary caused attrition.

**Recommendation:**  
Compare salary-band attrition rates and review compensation at job-role level.

---

## 14. Lower-Income, Higher-Attrition Roles Need Review

**Insight:**  
Sales Representatives and Laboratory Technicians appear in the lower-income, higher-attrition area of the role analysis.

**Interpretation:**  
These roles combine compensation concerns with elevated retention risk.

**Recommendation:**  
Review pay, workload, promotion opportunities and external market competitiveness for these roles.

---
